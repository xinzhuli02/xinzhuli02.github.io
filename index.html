About Me

Hello! My name is Winter(Xinzhu) Li.

My academic and professional journey has been shaped by an unusually interdisciplinary path, spanning language and literature, education, information science, archives, and artificial intelligence. Rather than approaching technology from a purely technical perspective, I have always been interested in the human side of information: how people create meaning, share knowledge, and interact with systems that shape their understanding of the world.

Across these different fields, I have found myself returning to the same set of questions: How do information structures influence interpretation? How do technologies shape learning and decision-making? And how can systems be designed to better support human agency, creativity, and understanding?

My experiences have taken me across classrooms, research projects, archives, and technology-related work. Although these environments are quite different, they all involve questions about how people learn, communicate, and make sense of information. This perspective continues to shape the way I approach both research and practice.

Outside of my professional interests, I enjoy piano, photography, golf, swimming, and travel. I am always looking for new experiences, whether that means learning a skill, exploring a new place, or spending time outdoors.

Research Assistant — SCAT Testing and Creative Spatial Problem Solving

Center for Talented Youth, Johns Hopkins University Baltimore, USA · Apr 2022 – Feb 2023
  • Collaborated with the SCAT testing department to prepare materials, organize administration workshops, and monitor scoring and scaling procedures.
  • Conducted trend analyses and comparative studies of test outcomes; contributed to statistical report preparation for cognitive assessment research.
  • Worked with the creative spatial assessment team on “Creative Spatial Problem Solving,” exploring links between spatial reasoning and academic ability using neurocognitive tasks such as mental rotation, cube/figure turning, and box folding/unfolding.

Research Assistant — The Influence of Culture on People's Self-evaluation

Advisor: Professor Cheng Liao, Harvard University Cambridge, USA · Mar 2022 – Sep 2023
  • Investigated how cultural background influences individuals’ self-perception and evaluation of personal ability and worth.
  • Analyzed cross-cultural psychological studies to identify variations in self-esteem and self-criticism between individualistic and collectivist societies.
  • Concluded that Western participants typically exhibit higher self-esteem and self-identity, while Eastern participants demonstrate more self-critical and modest self-evaluations.

Research Assistant — Intercultural Communication and Global Vision

Tsinghua University — Advisor: Professor Pang Hongmei Beijing, China · Mar 2020 – Apr 2020
  • Analyzed differences in media framing between China Daily and The New York Times during the COVID-19 pandemic to understand cultural attitudes toward mask-wearing.
  • Examined how individualistic versus collectivist cultural values shape public compliance and perceptions of health mandates.
  • Concluded that the U.S. emphasized individual freedom and voluntary compliance, whereas China’s collectivist culture favored collective responsibility and mandatory mask policies.
